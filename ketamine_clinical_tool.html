Important: Do Not Swallow

When using sublingual troches or rapid dissolve tablets, swallowing the accumulated saliva is not acceptable. Many clients are simply never told this. Swallowing produces 16-30x higher urinary metabolite concentrations and significantly increases bladder and bile duct exposure. Every client must be explicitly instructed: spit the saliva throughout — do not swallow it.

ADDITIONAL CLINICAL CAVEATS
CONSIDER GOING UP
Heavy regular alcohol use
Chronic alcohol upregulates NMDA receptors. Clients who drink heavily may metabolize ketamine faster and respond less at standard doses.
Subtherapeutic response at prior dose
If previous sessions were well tolerated but effect was minimal, a modest upward adjustment beyond the calculator may be warranted.
Previous session shorter than expected
If the session lasted significantly less than typical — under 30 min for IM/SQ or under 45 min for sublingual/IN — this may indicate rapid CYP2B6 metabolism. Consider a modest upward adjustment and document the response.
CONSIDER CANDIDACY
Psychosis or schizophrenia spectrum
Personal history of schizophrenia, schizoaffective disorder, schizotypal personality disorder, or any active psychosis are hard contraindications. These clients are not appropriate candidates for our medical practice under any circumstances. Ketamine is a dissociative NMDA antagonist and can precipitate or worsen psychotic symptoms. Do not proceed.
Bipolar I or active mania
Active mania is a hard contraindication. Bipolar I disorder, even when stable and medicated, requires a letter of clearance and active treatment relationship with a prescribing psychiatrist before proceeding. Bipolar II warrants the same coordination. Ketamine can trigger manic episodes.
Family history of psychosis or schizophrenia
First-degree family history of schizophrenia or psychosis represents a genetic vulnerability. A retreat or home setting without real-time monitoring is not appropriate. Consider referring to an IV ketamine clinic where the infusion can be stopped immediately if symptoms emerge.
Liver impairment
Significant hepatic impairment is a contraindication. Clients with active hepatitis, cirrhosis, or markedly elevated LFTs are not appropriate candidates for our medical practice. Medical clearance from a specialist is required before any consideration.
Cardiovascular history
Uncontrolled hypertension, significant arrhythmia, or CAD are contraindications for a retreat or home setting. These clients are not appropriate candidates for our medical practice. Consider referring to a facility with cardiac monitoring and IV access.
CNS depressants on board — session not held as instructed
If gabapentin, sleep meds, antipsychotics, or benzos were not held as recommended, consider postponing rather than proceeding.
ABSOLUTE CONTRAINDICATION — MEDICATIONS
MAOIs — Do Not Proceed
Phenelzine (Nardil), tranylcypromine (Parnate), selegiline (Emsam), isocarboxazid (Marplan). The combination with ketamine carries risk of serotonin syndrome, which can be fatal. This is the only medication that is a hard absolute contraindication. No exceptions. If a client is on an MAOI, do not schedule, do not proceed, and refer to their prescribing physician for transition planning.
MEDICATIONS TO REVIEW BEFORE SESSION
Hold before session
Stimulants (Adderall, Vyvanse, Ritalin)
Hold day-of. Raises cardiovascular risk.
Tramadol
Hold 24+ hours. Lowers seizure threshold and opioid interaction risk.
High-dose opioids
Discuss with prescribing physician. CNS depression stacking.
Naltrexone
Hold minimum 24 hours. Blocks opioid receptor pathways that ketamine partially uses.
Coordinate with prescriber — do not hold without guidance
Benzodiazepines
Additive CNS depression, blunts ketamine effect. Ideally hold, but taper risk must be weighed with prescriber.
Antipsychotics
Additive sedation. Raises question of why they are prescribed — coordinate before proceeding.
Gabapentin / Lyrica
Additive CNS depression. Hold if possible, coordinate with prescriber.
Sleep medications
Hold night before and day-of session.
Muscle relaxants
Additive sedation. Hold day-of.
GLP-1 agonists (Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro)
Nausea stacking with ketamine is significant, especially with sublingual and intranasal routes.
MONITOR — GENERALLY COMPATIBLE
Continue with awareness
SSRIs / SNRIs
Generally compatible. No need to hold.
Lamotrigine / Lamictal
May blunt ketamine's antidepressant effect. Worth documenting and discussing with client.
Lithium
Not a contraindication. Emerging evidence suggests possible synergy with ketamine's antidepressant effect. Coordinate with prescribing psychiatrist and monitor BP and neurological response during session. A client on lithium may be psychologically complex and could benefit from a monitored IV clinic setting rather than a retreat or home environment.
Mood stabilizers (Depakote, Tegretol, Trileptal)
Generally compatible. Coordinate with prescribing psychiatrist before proceeding. Clients on mood stabilizers are often managing significant psychiatric complexity — consider whether a retreat or home setting is appropriate or whether a monitored IV clinic setting would better serve them.
Blood pressure medications
Generally compatible. Monitor BP before and at 20-30 min — ketamine raises BP.
Thyroid medications
Generally compatible. No interaction of concern.
Clinical Reminder

This tool provides a starting point based on weight-based dosing protocol. Always apply clinical judgment. Check blood pressure before dosing and at 20-30 minutes. Consult your supervising physician for any client outside standard parameters.
