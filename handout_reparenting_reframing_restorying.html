Re-Parenting, Reframing,
and Re-Storying

How new stories settle into the spaces where old stories lived, and the healing practices that make that possible

The nervous system does not know the difference between something real and something imagined. That is not a limitation. It is the doorway.

The Foundation
🧠
The Nervous System Cannot Fact-Check
Neuroscience
This is the foundational principle that makes re-parenting, reframing, and re-storying possible. The nervous system does not know the difference between something that is happening and something that is vividly imagined. It responds to perception, not reality. A memory triggers the same physiological cascade as the original event. A feared scenario produces the same stress hormones as an actual threat. A deeply imagined experience of being held, seen, and safe produces the same neurological response as actually being held, seen, and safe.

This is why hypnosis works. It is why guided imagery works. It is why a skilled guide who sits with you in a hard moment and offers something genuinely different, attunement instead of dismissal, safety instead of threat, calm instead of chaos, can begin to update what the nervous system believes to be true.

The nervous system learns from experience. And with the right support, a new experience, even an imagined or facilitated one, is experience enough to begin the update.
This is also why unprocessed trauma keeps replaying. The nervous system is not broken. It is doing exactly what it was designed to do: treat the past as if it is still present, because no one has yet given it a new experience to learn from.
Old Stories Are Body Memories
📖
Where Old Stories Live
The Setup
Old stories are not just beliefs. They are body memories, nervous system defaults, automatic responses that fire before thought can intervene. They were written early, in environments where we had no power to question them, and they got treated as truth because they were the only version of reality available.

The story that says you are too much. The story that says you do not matter. The story that says love is conditional, safety is temporary, and the only way to belong is to make yourself useful. These are not intellectual positions. They live in the posture, in the breath, in the way the body contracts when someone gets too close or the room gets too quiet.

You cannot argue your way out of a body memory. You cannot think your way out of a nervous system pattern. The story has to be met at the level it was written, which is the level of felt experience. New information alone does not update these defaults. New experience does.
Most of the stories helpers carry were written before they had words. They were absorbed from the environment, from the expressions on faces, from what was and was not responded to. They are not your fault. They were your best attempt at making sense of the world you were given.
Re-Parenting
🤲
Giving Yourself What You Did Not Receive
The Practice
Re-parenting is the practice of consciously offering yourself the experiences your nervous system needed but never received. The safety. The attunement. The calm, present witness who does not need anything from you. The permission to be small, uncertain, and not yet okay.

Many helpers were shaped into caretakers before they were ever shaped into selves. They learned to read the room before they learned to read their own needs. They learned that love was something you earned by being useful, not something you simply received for existing. Re-parenting is the slow, deliberate practice of rewriting that contract.

It can happen in many ways. Through a skilled guide who models the attunement you never had. Through somatic practices that teach the body to receive care instead of only giving it. Through intentional inner child work, where the adult self turns toward the younger self and offers what was absent. Through medicine experiences that dissolve the walls long enough for that contact to actually happen.

Re-parenting does not require your original parents to change or acknowledge anything. It requires only that you become willing to be the one who shows up differently for the part of you that is still waiting.
Helpers often find re-parenting the most uncomfortable of all the healing practices because it requires receiving. Giving feels safe. Receiving feels like exposure. That discomfort is information. It points directly to where the work lives.
Reframing
🖼️
Was the Original Story Ever True?
The Question
Reframing is the cognitive layer of this work. It begins with one deceptively simple question: was the original story ever accurate, or was it the only interpretation a young and powerless person could construct from the available evidence?

A child who is not protected concludes that they are not worth protecting. A child who is not seen concludes that they are not worth seeing. A child whose emotional needs are dismissed concludes that their emotions are a problem. These conclusions made sense given the information available at the time. They were the most logical explanation a developing mind could produce.

They were also incomplete. The child did not have access to the other explanation: that the adults around them were doing the best they could with broken tools, and that the failure of care was not a verdict on the child's worth.

Reframing does not minimize the harm. It does not ask you to forgive before you are ready or to pretend things were different than they were. It simply holds open the possibility that the story you were handed is not the only story that fits the facts, and that you are now old enough to choose a different interpretation.
The nervous system will resist this. The old story feels like truth because it was true for a long time. Reframing is not a one-time decision. It is a practice, and it requires repetition before the new frame begins to feel as solid as the old one did.
Re-Storying
✍️
Letting New Stories Settle In
Integration
Re-storying is what happens when re-parenting and reframing go deep enough to become felt truth rather than intellectual concept. It is the integration of both into a new narrative that does not just exist in the mind but settles into the spaces the old story inhabited.

This is important. New stories do not park beside old ones and wait to be chosen. They need to move in. They need to take up residence in the body, in the breath, in the automatic responses that used to carry the old script. This takes time, repetition, and a quality of attention that most people were never taught to bring to themselves.

Re-storying sounds like: "I was shaped to be a caretaker, and that is also a strength. It no longer has to be the whole of me." It sounds like: "I needed things that were not given to me. That speaks to a failure of my environment, not to my worth." It sounds like: "I am allowed to be complex, imperfect, uncertain, and still fully worthy of care."

Re-storying is not denial of the past. It is the conscious authorship of what comes next.
Wild and wise and finally, finally whole. That is what re-storying looks like when it works. Not the erasure of what came before, but the arrival at something larger that holds it all.
The Role of Hypnosis and Guided Imagery
The nervous system does not fact-check the source of an experience.
Hypnosis works for exactly the reason outlined above. A deeply relaxed nervous system in a hypnotic state accepts new experiences as real. A skilled hypnotherapist or guide can facilitate the experience of being held, protected, witnessed, and responded to in exactly the ways the nervous system needed and never received, and the body responds as if it is actually happening.

This is not manipulation. It is the deliberate use of the nervous system's own learning mechanism. The same mechanism that wrote the wound can be used to heal it. Guided imagery, somatic repair practices, and medicine-supported healing all operate through this same doorway, offering the nervous system new experiences vivid and felt enough to begin updating the old ones.
Medicine Work and Re-Storying
✨
The Medicine Window
Accelerated Healing
Medicine work, when held with skill and intention, opens a window in which the nervous system becomes temporarily more permeable. The rigid defenses that keep old stories locked in place soften. The gap between the rational mind and the felt body narrows. New experiences, new frames, new felt senses of safety and worth have a chance to land at a depth that ordinary waking consciousness rarely allows.

A skilled guide working in this window can do re-parenting work in real time. They tune in to the moment when an old story surfaces and gently, precisely offer something different. Not a lecture. Not analysis. A quality of presence, a reflection, a witnessing, a calm that says: you are safe here, you are seen here, this is allowed. The nervous system receives that and files it as experience.

This is why the guide matters as much as the medicine. The medicine opens the window. The guide determines what walks through it. A skilled guide can use that window to offer the relational repair that re-parenting requires, at the precise moment the nervous system is most ready to receive it.
Integration after medicine work is where the new story gets written down. What happened in the session needs to be processed, spoken, felt again, and woven into the ongoing narrative of who you are becoming. Without integration, the window closes and the experience fades. With it, the new story has a chance to settle in and stay.

The 48 hours after a session are especially important. Nature, even a short walk outside, helps complete the grounding the session began. Journaling allows the insights and images that surfaced to find language before they fade. Music chosen with intention can hold the emotional tone of what opened and keep it alive a little longer. Time with animals offers uncomplicated co-regulation, warmth without agenda. Nourishing whole food supports a nervous system that has done significant work. Gentle movement, stretching, yoga, and somatic practices help the body continue processing what the mind has begun to understand. Creative expression, drawing, coloring, painting, working with clay, or any tactile making bypasses the analytical mind and lets the experience find form without language. Intentional breathwork helps the body complete what the medicine started. Prioritizing rest and sleep matters more than most people realize, as the nervous system does profound processing work during sleep. If there is one trusted person in your life who can hold what happened without judgment or agenda, speaking the experience aloud helps it integrate. Equally important is protecting the space from what disrupts it, because screens, news, social media, alcohol, and overstimulation in the days after a session can close the integration window before it has done its work. The goal is not to analyze the experience. The goal is to stay close to it, gently, and let it continue doing its work.
🔄
Your Medicine Guide Can Help You Re-Story
The Relationship
A medicine guide who understands re-parenting and re-storying is not just a safety monitor. They are an active participant in the healing process. They know how to hold space in a way that communicates: your feelings are welcome here, your needs are not too much, you do not have to earn your place in this room.

In a session, this might look like a hand placed gently on the back when the body is shaking. A voice that is steady when the experience becomes overwhelming. A presence that does not flinch, does not fix, does not rush. For someone whose nervous system was wired in environments where they had to manage everyone else's response to their own pain, being in the presence of someone who genuinely does not need them to perform or manage is itself a re-parenting experience.

Over time, and across sessions, this builds. The nervous system accumulates evidence that a different kind of relational experience is possible. The old story loses some of its certainty. The new one begins to feel real.

This is the work. It is not fast, but it is real. And it changes things at the level they need to be changed, which is not the level of understanding, but the level of felt experience.
For Helpers Specifically
💛
Shaped Into Caretakers Before Being Shaped Into Selves
The Helpers Wound
Many helpers did not choose helping as a career. They were trained for it long before they knew what a career was. They learned to read the emotional temperature of the room before they learned to read their own needs. They learned that love was conditional on performance, that their value was in their usefulness, that taking up space was a risk, and that the safest way to belong was to make themselves necessary.

The helping profession was not a departure from the original wound. It was the most socially sanctioned expression of it.

Re-parenting for helpers often means learning to receive rather than only give. It means practicing the radical and uncomfortable act of having needs, expressing them, and allowing someone else to respond. It means tolerating the feeling of being cared for without immediately deflecting, minimizing, or earning it back through service.

Reframing for helpers often means questioning the story that their worth lives in their function. That they are only valuable when they are needed. That rest is laziness, vulnerability is weakness, and asking for help is a burden placed on others.

Re-storying for helpers is the slow building of a narrative in which they are a person, not just a role. In which their feelings matter not because someone granted them permission, but because they are human. In which the care they have given so generously to others is finally, finally turned inward.
You cannot give from an empty place indefinitely. Every helper eventually meets the wall. Re-parenting is not self-indulgence. It is the restoration of the source. It is the work that makes sustainable, genuine helping possible.
✦ A Note for Helpers
The old story was not the truth. It was a conclusion drawn by someone very young, with very limited information, in circumstances they had no power to change. It made sense at the time. It kept you safe at the time. It is no longer required.

New stories are possible. The nervous system is not fixed. The body is not a museum of your wounds. It is a living system that learns, updates, and changes in response to new experience, and there is no age at which that capacity disappears.

The work is real. The healing is real. The window, once opened, can change everything. What you allow yourself to receive in that window, and in the ordinary moments between sessions, is what determines what gets written next.

You are allowed to rewrite this. You are allowed to become someone whose story ends differently than it began.
