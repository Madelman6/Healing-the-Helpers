Films About Psychedelics and Consciousness
Content Notice
Several films in this collection contain depictions of trauma, mental health crises, grief, and suicide. Please care for yourself as you explore these resources.
1.An Act of Service A New York Times Op-Doc short film following Rob, a fire captain, through ketamine-assisted psychotherapy for PTSD. He spent years refusing to acknowledge what the job had done to him. This film shows what happens when a first responder finally lets the medicine in, and why the culture of silence in the fire service costs lives. Free on YouTube and essential viewing for anyone who works with or loves a first responder.
2.Lighter: A Ketamine Experience A one-hour documentary following three people through ketamine therapy for depression, anxiety, trauma, and addiction, including a Navy veteran who healed from PTSD and suicidality after years of failed conventional treatment. Intimate, honest, and beautifully made. If you are curious about what ketamine-assisted healing actually looks and feels like from the inside, start here.
3.Have a Good Trip: Adventures in Psychedelics A Netflix documentary where celebrities share their most memorable psychedelic experiences through interviews, animations, and dramatic re-enactments. Equal parts hilarious and genuinely moving. A surprisingly good entry point for people who are curious but not sure where to start, and a reminder that psychedelics have always been part of the cultural conversation.
4.A Table of Our Own Women in the psychedelic space share their stories of healing, leadership, and reclaiming their voices in a field that has historically centered men. Practitioners, researchers, advocates, and healers on what it means to bring feminine wisdom into medicine work. Deeply relevant for any woman navigating this world as a provider or as a client.
5.Aya A female-centered documentary following women through ayahuasca ceremonies in the Amazon and the profound healing that unfolds. One of the few psychedelic films that centers women's experiences specifically. Raw, intimate, and quietly powerful. Free on YouTube.
6.Fantastic Fungi Paul Stamets, Michael Pollan, and others explore the extraordinary intelligence of fungi, their role in connecting ecosystems, and their profound healing potential for humans. Visually stunning and scientifically rigorous. One of the most important and accessible entry points into understanding why psilocybin works. Watch this before any other film on this list.
7.Aaron Rodgers: Enigma A 3-part Netflix docuseries following NFL quarterback Aaron Rodgers through ayahuasca, plant medicine, and a spiritual transformation that changed how he sees himself and his life. What makes this worth watching is not the celebrity but the honesty. He talks about childhood wounds, identity, and what it means to finally stop performing and start healing. Relevant for anyone who has ever had to be the strong one.
8.The Sunshine Makers The wild, true story of Nick Sands and Tim Scully, the two chemists who produced the majority of the world's LSD in the 1960s and genuinely believed they were catalyzing a spiritual awakening for all of humanity. Their laboratory was located in Windsor, right here in Sonoma County. Part history, part character study, part love letter to an era when psychedelics felt like the answer to everything. Essential context for understanding where this movement came from.
9.Bad Trip Advice Practical, compassionate, and surprisingly funny guidance on navigating difficult psychedelic experiences. Covers what to do, what not to do, and how to support someone through a hard journey. Essential viewing for guides, trip sitters, and anyone who might someday find themselves sitting with another person during a difficult experience.
10.The Twelve An intimate portrait of twelve people before, during, and after transformative psychedelic experiences. The film stays close to the human story rather than the science, showing what integration actually looks like in real lives. Quiet, unhurried, and deeply moving. Available through Le Ciel Foundation.
11.Dreamshadow A documentary exploring ibogaine treatment for addiction, following veterans and civilians through one of the most powerful and demanding healing experiences available. Ibogaine works differently from other psychedelics, diving deep into the roots of addiction and trauma in a way that is not always comfortable to watch. Important for anyone working with people who have tried everything else.
12.Dosed One woman's journey using psilocybin mushrooms to heal from opioid addiction after years of failed conventional treatment. Shot over several years and unflinchingly honest about what addiction looks like, what healing looks like, and the gap between them. One of the most human psychedelic documentaries ever made.
13.Ayahuasca Diaries Personal accounts from diverse people who traveled to the Amazon seeking ayahuasca ceremonies and what they actually found, in the ceremony and in themselves afterward. Honest about both the profound and the difficult. A grounded look at what it means to seek healing in a foreign tradition and integrate it into a Western life.
14.From Shock to Awe Two combat veterans with severe PTSD, suicidal ideation, and shattered families turn to plant medicine after conventional treatment fails them. What follows is not a cure but a door opening into something neither of them expected. Deeply moving and unflinching, this film was made for and by the veteran community and it shows. Required viewing for anyone working with first responders or military.
15.Joe Dispenza: Source Follows participants through Dr. Joe Dispenza's advanced workshops, capturing the neuroscience of meditation, the power of coherent group energy, and the spontaneous physical and emotional healings that occur when people learn to change their brain states. Skeptics may struggle with parts of it. Open-minded viewers will find something genuinely worth sitting with.
16.Bufo Alvarius: The Underground Secret The most powerful documentary on 5-MeO-DMT, the medicine secreted by the Sonoran Desert toad and one of the most potent substances known to alter human consciousness. Features Stanislav Grof and shaman Octavio Rettig alongside stunning visuals inspired by the experience itself. Not for the faint of heart, and not meant to be. An intense meditation on what consciousness actually is and what it means to dissolve into it completely.
17.How to Change Your Mind Michael Pollan's landmark book brought to life as a four-part Netflix docuseries, one episode each on LSD, psilocybin, MDMA, and mescaline including Huachuma (San Pedro) and peyote. Pollan embeds himself in each medicine's world and tries it himself, reporting back with the curiosity of a journalist and the honesty of someone genuinely changed by what he found. The best mainstream introduction to the full landscape of psychedelic healing.
18.Hamilton's Pharmacopeia Chemist and journalist Hamilton Morris travels the world across three seasons investigating obscure psychedelic compounds, underground chemists, indigenous traditions, and the subcultures built around altered states. More edgy and less polished than other documentaries on this list, which is exactly the point. For the genuinely curious who want to go deeper than the mainstream conversation.
19.Aware: Glimpses of Consciousness A quiet, beautifully made documentary exploring the mystery of consciousness through the eyes of scientists, philosophers, meditators, and psychedelic researchers. Not about any particular medicine, but about the question that underlies all of this work: what is the mind, and what are we when we are not identified with it? One of the more contemplative films on this list and worth watching slowly.
20.Journeys to the Edge of Consciousness A beautifully animated documentary recreating the first psychedelic experiences of three intellectual giants, Timothy Leary, Aldous Huxley, and Alan Watts. Historically grounded and visually stunning, it traces how three very different minds encountered the same territory and came back with entirely different maps. Essential for anyone who wants to understand the philosophical roots of the modern psychedelic renaissance.
21.Dirty Pictures Portrait of Alexander Shulgin, the eccentric genius who synthesized over 200 psychedelic compounds in his home laboratory and introduced MDMA to the psychotherapy world. A love story, a scientific biography, and a meditation on what drives a person to spend a lifetime mapping inner space. One of the most important figures in psychedelic history, and one of the least known. This film changes that.

Films About Trauma, Mental Health, and Being Human
Content Notice
These films deal directly with trauma, mental health crises, OCD, grief, and suicide. They are powerful precisely because they do not look away. Please tend to yourself while watching.
1.Lie to Fly A New York Times documentary following Joseph Emerson, an off-duty pilot who experienced a severe mental health crisis mid-flight two days after taking psilocybin mushrooms while grieving the sudden death of his best friend. The film goes beyond the incident itself to examine the culture of silence around mental health in aviation and what happens when helpers cannot ask for help. Essential viewing for anyone working with first responders.
2.#SkyKing A documentary about Richard "Beebo" Russell, a 28-year-old airport ground service agent who stole a passenger plane from Seattle-Tacoma Airport and flew it solo through the Pacific Northwest for over an hour before intentionally crashing it. Includes never-before-heard ATC audio and his family speaking publicly for the first time. A devastating and empathetic portrait of an undiagnosed man in a mental health crisis with nowhere to turn, a mirror held up to the working-class helpers America forgets.
3.Addition Starring Teresa Palmer as Grace, a mathematician whose OCD stems from a childhood trauma and manifests as an uncontrollable compulsion to count everything around her. A romantic drama that treats mental health with genuine compassion, one of the most honest portrayals of anxiety, OCD, and the way we hide our struggles from the people we love. Healing is not linear, and this film knows it.
4.This Is Me Now: A Love Story Jennifer Lopez traces the patterns of toxic relationships and unresolved trauma through her own eyes, weaving therapy sessions, dreamlike visuals, and raw honesty about the cycles we repeat until we heal. Simplistic and visually bold, some will find it deeply moving, others will find it wildly over the top. Either way it is worth watching. On Amazon Prime Video.
5.The Gentle Art of Swedish Death Cleaning A Peacock series narrated by Amy Poehler where three Swedish practitioners including an organizer, a designer, and a psychologist who help Americans face mortality by letting go of belongings. Based on the Swedish practice of döstädning, the idea of clearing your life so those you love are not left to do it for you. Surprisingly moving, genuinely funny, and a gentle doorway into thinking about death before it arrives.

6.This Is Your Mind on Plants Michael Pollan · shorter and more accessible than How to Change Your Mind, focused on opium, caffeine, and mescaline
7.The Psychedelic Experience Timothy Leary, Ralph Metzner, and Richard Alpert · the original 1964 guide based on the Tibetan Book of the Dead · foundational and still essential
8.DMT: The Spirit Molecule Rick Strassman, MD · the research behind endogenous DMT and what it reveals about consciousness
9.When the Impossible Happens Stanislav Grof, MD · his most readable book, written as personal stories rather than academic theory, covering fifty years of extraordinary research into LSD, Holotropic Breathwork, consciousness after death, and the full landscape of non-ordinary reality · the best entry point into his work
10.How Psychedelics Work: The Hidden Mind and Our Power to Heal Robin Carhart-Harris · brand new 2025 · the world's leading psychedelic neuroscientist's first book, covering the brain science, the research, and the future of the field

Books About Trauma, Mental Health, and Being Human

10.Waking the Tiger Peter A. Levine, PhD · the book that introduced Somatic Experiencing to the world, translated into 22 languages, explaining how the body holds trauma and how physical sensation and movement can release it when talk therapy cannot · the foundational somatic trauma text

12.Final Gifts: Understanding the Special Awareness, Needs, and Communications of the Dying Maggie Callanan and Patricia Kelley · Two hospice nurses share over twenty years of intimate experience with the dying, revealing the extraordinary ways people communicate their needs and feelings in their final days and the wisdom, love, and gifts they leave behind
